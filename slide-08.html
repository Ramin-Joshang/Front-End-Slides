Presenter Notes

Teaching Intent: To demystify the "Cloud" by showing it as a physical network of connected hardware. We want students to understand that data travels a physical distance.

Speech Script: "When we talk about 'The Internet,' it’s easy to think of it as a magical cloud floating above us. But in reality, it’s a massive, physical network of networks. Think of it as the world's largest shipping system. On one end, you have your device—the 'Client.' When you ask for a website, your request is broken into tiny pieces called 'Data Packets.' These packets don't just fly through the air; they travel through your home router, into fiber-optic cables buried in the ground, and sometimes even through massive undersea cables stretching across oceans. Finally, they reach a Data Center—a giant building full of powerful computers called Servers. The Internet is the road system; the Web is just one of the many types of traffic that uses those roads."

Transition: "Now that we see the map, let’s introduce the three main characters you'll be working with every day as a developer."

Animation Cue: 1. Fade in the Network Map group to show the physical flow. 2. Use 'Appear' for the three info cards at the bottom to define 'Packets', 'Routing', and the 'Global Web' distinction.
